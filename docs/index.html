    How they learned to fight

    Nobody programmed a punch. Each fly carries the same wiring diagram: neurons in 21 real fruit-fly cell types joined by synapses. What differs is the genome, the strength of every one of those synapses.

    Each generation, flies fight their rivals, past champions and a scripted sparring partner. The ones that land lunges and avoid taking them breed, and their offspring mutate. Approach, aim, lunge timing, guarding and the giant-fiber escape hop all emerged on their own.

    Real male fruit flies do fight over food and mates: they lunge, flick their wings as a threat and rear up to strike with their forelegs. This ring just gives them gloves.
