The brain

  • Eyes: LC10 columns see where the rival is, LC4 sees it looming, LC16 catches a sudden lunge.
  • Nose & body: Or67d neurons smell the rival's cVA pheromone, bristles feel the ropes, mechanosensors feel a hit.
  • Central brain: P1 aggression neurons, central-complex steering (PFL3, fan-shaped body), lateral horn and mushroom body.
  • Descending neurons drive the body: DNa02 turns, DNp09 walks, MDN backs up, the giant fiber triggers an escape hop.

Orange wires excite, violet wires inhibit (one transmitter per neuron, like a real fly).

How they learned

No one programmed a single punch. Each fly's genome is the strength of every synapse in the wiring diagram. Flies fight rivals, past champions and a sparring bot; the ones that land lunges and avoid taking them breed, and their offspring mutate. Over generations the population discovers approach, aim, lunge timing, guarding and dodging on its own.

Real male fruit flies really do fight: they lunge, wing-threat and even rear up and "box" with their forelegs. This ring just gives them gloves.
